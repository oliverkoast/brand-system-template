# Northlight — Voice & Copy Guide

> section headings are the transferable part.

How the brand writes. Claude reads this whenever it writes or edits copy. It governs **voice**
(how we say it), not **facts** (what we say). Real details always come from the person.

## The voice in one line

Plain, specific, unhurried. Says the useful thing first.

## Tone principles

- **Lead with the answer.** The context can come second if it is needed at all.
- **Concrete over abstract.** Name the thing, the number, the date.
- **Confident, not loud.** No superlatives doing the work a fact should do.
- **No hype vocabulary.** Nothing is revolutionary, game-changing, or seamless.

## Rhythm

- Headlines are **3 to 7 words**.
- Body mixes short sentences with one longer one. Read it aloud; if you run out of breath, cut it.
- **No em-dashes.** Use commas, colons, or parentheses.

## Calls to action

Invitational and specific: "See the specimen", "Read the contract". Never "Learn more", which
tells the reader nothing about where they are going.

## What never gets invented

- Names, dates, figures, prices, quotes, claims about results.
- Anything characterising a third party's behaviour.

If a detail is missing, use a labeled placeholder and ask. Never invent one to fill a line.
That is the no-invent rule, applied to copy.
